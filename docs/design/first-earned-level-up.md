## One controlled encounter

| Setting | Proposed test value and reason |
| --- | --- |
| Roster/view | Fixed one or two players, one first-person rifle each; no joining, leaving or weapon replacement during a run. |
| Arena/threats | Existing graybox arena, seed 137, 35 approaching drones total in both solo and pair tests. Drones respawn at the perimeter after death. Keep current movement/pose/precision rules. In a pair, target the nearest living player each simulation tick, lower stable player ID on an exact tie; ignore dead players. |
| Player start | 100 HP each, initial positions at arena centre offset ±2 metres on X for a pair, centre for solo; default view. Contact deals 10, with the existing 0.4-second grace tracked separately per player. No friendly damage or player obstruction of rifle queries in this test. |
| Rifle start | 20 body / 40 head damage; 0.12-second interval (8.333… shots/second); 65-metre range; current automatic firing. Disable rate edits in this experiment; preserve the existing baseline and benchmark modes. |
| Drone HP | **60 in this experiment only.** With the accepted 40-HP control, +50% damage changes neither body nor head hit count. At 60 HP, the proposed damage choice changes both, giving the comparison an observable threshold. HP, spawn pressure and movement stay identical across upgrade comparisons. |
| Duration | 60 seconds of active encounter time, shared by the party; survive to the end with at least one living player to complete. Final game duration/structure stays open. |
| Exposure | Start team level 1, 0 XP; level 2 at 10 collected XP, one personal choice entitlement per roster member. This fixture ends progression at level 2, explicitly replacing the older two-choice exposure target for this experiment. Later collected XP remains visible in a cumulative total but grants no further levels in this fixture. No automatic/time-based choice. |

The threshold is identical for solo/pair; a pair may earn it sooner because both contribute. This is a controlled exposure hypothesis, not selected party-size scaling. Aim to earn the choice around 10–25 seconds and leave at least 20 seconds to use it. If normal play cannot do that, record the failure and propose one tuning change; do not secretly grant XP or extrapolate to three/four players. One rifle and one choice isolate growth before expanding stacking.

## Pickup to shared level

1. Each authoritative transition from positive enemy HP to zero creates **one orb worth 1 XP** at that incarnation's death position, projected onto the flat arena floor. Use the death snapshot before reusing the enemy slot. A kill alone grants no XP; a fresh incarnation's later death is a separate event.
2. A living player collects an orb when their ground-plane centre is within **2 metres**, inclusive. Orbs remain stationary until collected or the run ends; no attraction, magnet, expiry, aggregation or range upgrade in this fixture. Future collection effects retain their own requirements. Dead players cannot collect.
3. The authoritative encounter consumes each orb once. Overlapping collectors choose the lower stable player ID for collection attribution; both still receive the same team benefit. Repeated messages cannot credit the orb twice. Collection source is not damage/kill credit.
4. At cumulative collected XP ≥10, advance the shared level once to 2 and issue one uniquely identified entitlement to **each fixed roster member, alive or dead**. The dead player's participation rule is a test proposal; it does not spend another player's choice or give free gear. No per-player XP ladder, kill-credit bonus or collector bonus.
5. Show team level/XP and an unobtrusive "Level 2 — choose your rifle upgrade" cue. At level 2, show "Test level cap" and cumulative collected XP, rather than a misleading next-level bar. Log death, orb, collector, XP and entitlement events against the run ID.

## Personal offers and contrasting effects

Each player's entitlement generates a concrete offer once, using that player's random stream and current legal targets. The fixture has **two ordinary candidates and two distinct cards**, sampled without replacement with equal weight. Thus both players see the same two definitions, potentially in different order, with their own offer IDs and ownership. This small pool tests independent selection/application and overlapping offers; it cannot establish interesting random pool variety or rarity probabilities. Consuming A's stream or choice must not consume B's stream, change B's card order or select for B.

| Working label / legal target | Rank-one effect | Intended visible difference against a fresh 60-HP drone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Stronger hits — your starting rifle** | Multiply that rifle's base direct damage by 1.5: body 20→30, head 40→60. Keep head ×2 and interval 0.12 seconds. | Three body hits→two, two head hits→one. Fewer confirmed hits per target, more value from an exposed head; cadence unchanged. |
| **Faster fire — your starting rifle** | Multiply that rifle's rate by 1.5: interval 0.12→0.08 seconds (8.333…→12.5/s). Keep body 20 / head 40. | Same three body/two head hits, with shorter gaps, more frequent shots and more opportunities to redirect fire. |

Both enhance existing direct damage or firing cadence; neither adds piercing, ricochet, area damage or a new weapon. They have the same nominal +50% continuous damage throughput, but discrete kills, aim, misses, overkill and shared targets change practical usefulness. No equal-power or equal-fun claim follows. With time measured from the first hit, all-body kills take 0.24 seconds before growth, 0.12 with stronger hits, or 0.16 with faster fire; all-head kills take 0.12, 0, or 0.08 seconds respectively. Human perception still needs testing.

There is one rank and no repeats, rerolls, skips that spend the entitlement, rank stacking or transformations in this fixture. The unselected card does not apply. Both require the owned starting rifle and an unspent entitlement. Closing/reopening preserves the offer; do not reroll on death/revival or teammate selection. Revalidate the target on selection. Missing/replaced weapons or an empty pool violate this fixed-loadout fixture: reject without spending the choice and report the error; no silent substitute or trait grant. Future larger pools must specify those cases before use.

Display the rifle target, old/new body/head damage and interval on the cards. Apply only after an explicit selection, acknowledged once by the authoritative encounter, at the next simulation tick before that player's next due shot. Submitted repeats are idempotent. Prior shots and damage events keep their original snapshots. Faster fire preserves fractional cooldown progress, as the existing rate control does; it creates no retroactive catch-up burst. A dead player's effect updates the retained rifle and is active on return. A stale choice from a prior run or a terminal encounter is rejected without modifying state.

### Temporary selection presentation

Combat and the shared countdown continue while choices are visible. Use nonblocking cards away from the reticle, incoming-threat cues and wounded HP. With the cursor locked, **1/2 selects the corresponding visible card** and **Tab hides/reopens the cards**; selection keys are inactive when cards are hidden. Movement/aim remain available. There is no timeout, default/automatic choice, invulnerability or forced teammate selection. Closing preserves the earned choice until selection or the run ends.

This is an explicit temporary simplification to isolate growth without implementing GDJ-7. It may make reading choices under pressure unpleasant or inaccessible; record that result. The accepted future thinking space still banks choices, freezes physical combat for unlimited mental decision time and resumes through readiness. Live selection in this fixture does not replace that product rule. Escape/focus loss can release the local cursor and suspend that player's movement/firing input; it does not freeze the shared encounter, enemy damage, revival or countdown. The original solo control mode retains its pause behavior.

After application show the player's current rifle values and a brief factual confirmation, plus teammate status "A: stronger hits" / "B: faster fire" after their selections. Retain GDJ-17 white/green/gold aim state, independent hit/CRIT/KILL cues, damaged-only amber enemy HP and independent player damage cues. No fake amplified hit, heal, full-health bar or new lore name. Minimal A/B labels on teammate proxies identify their position; shoot effects retain source ownership. Players may choose the same card; ordinary choices never grant to the teammate.

## Death, ending and shared retry

| Situation | Proposed deterministic behavior |
| --- | --- |
| One player dies while another lives | Dead player stops movement, shots, contact targeting and pickup. Follow the living teammate's view; retain their build/offer and allow 1/2 choices. Start a **five-second active-time respawn timer**, unlimited returns in this fixture with no XP/build penalty. New team-level entitlement is still granted if the living teammate collects the threshold. |
| Return while teammate remains alive | Return at the player's initial spawn with 100 HP, retained upgrade/choice and **one second of visibly indicated contact immunity**. Freeze neither teammate nor encounter. A return without an upgrade has the original rifle. Death/revival research and production safety/penalties remain GDJ-13 work. |
| Everyone dead/waiting | Immediately set shared defeat, including solo and pending/expiring timers. No revival, pickup, offer generation or further upgrade application after this terminal transition. Existing choices/results can be displayed read-only. |
| 60 seconds reached with someone alive | Shared encounter complete; freeze encounter actions/timers. A dead teammate shares the result; no forced revival or extra choice. |
| Retry | Each roster member presses **Retry** to mark ready; show A/B readiness. When all are ready, start one new run for both. Solo starts on its one click. This all-roster acknowledgement is a test proposal, not a settled production readiness rule. |
| Connection loss | Abort the test for both with the reason recorded, rather than treating departure as death/success or silently reducing the roster. Reconnect/host migration/late joins require later rules; an abort is not a completed gameplay run. |

Use one authoritative 60-Hz encounter order: (1) validate/apply queued personal selections in stable player-ID order; (2) resolve due shots, deaths and contact damage, recording enemy death/orb snapshots before respawn; (3) evaluate all-dead defeat, then duration completion; (4) only if still running, process expiring revival timers; (5) collect orbs and issue team-level entitlements. New offers cannot be selected until a subsequent tick. The last player's death on the completion tick or a teammate's revival-expiry tick therefore causes defeat. Dead players earn through a living collector in a nonterminal tick. Choices accepted earlier in the tick retain their logged effect; nothing submitted after ending applies. Logical ordering does not select networking technology or guarantee display/network latency.

Retry resets **run ID, timer, player HP/positions/views/death/immunity state, both rifles to 20/40 and 0.12 seconds, enemy HP/positions/incarnations, orbs/consumed IDs, XP/level/entitlements, offers/selections/ranks/random streams, cooldowns, counters, feedback, pending messages and readiness**. Seed 137 and stable roster IDs remain for repeatable comparisons; reset personal random streams rather than letting a teammate's draw advance them. Resetting incarnation counters is safe only because the new run ID invalidates old events. Preserve display/audio/access settings. A surviving GDJ-19 rate preference is not the growth state: this test uses its fixed baseline on every retry, while the original control/benchmark modes preserve their preferences. R in a terminal test is the local Retry action; disable immediate in-combat R reset in this cooperative fixture. No persistent discovery/power/unlock is awarded by this experiment.

## Attribution and future boundaries

The authoritative encounter owns HP/death, orb consumption, shared XP/level and terminal/reset state. Each player's build, offer, choice and random sequence remain individually owned state within it. Upgrade definitions name effect/target/requirements; only direct damage and cadence effects are needed now. Team level, personal offer, effect application and presentation are separate events. Keep hooks for known player/pickup, loot and draw effects without building a universal modifier engine or encoding every upgrade as a rifle field.

Retain run ID, source player, source weapon instance, shot/event ID, enemy slot **and incarnation**, region, requested damage, actual damage and before/after HP. For a 60-HP target, A's 30-body hit followed by B's requested 40-head hit attributes 30 actual damage to each, one death and one orb; the last valid damaging source gets kill credit. An event aimed at an already-dead/reused incarnation cannot damage its replacement or award another kill. Collector credit stays separate, and shared XP never implies equal damage contribution. Existing snapshots are a starting point; current code has no complete source/authority model.

Qualifying rare content is **absent from the entire test pool**, including forced/control draws. This deliberately avoids unresolved qualifying tiers, incompatible recipients and shared grant bookkeeping; it does not redefine the accepted selection-triggered team grant/credit rule. GDJ-14 and a later dedicated test retain those questions. Thinking space, magnets/range upgrades, additional weapons, loot/draw modifiers, permanent progression, special combinations, cameras and lore are later work. Four-player tuning, dense orb/query/effect costs and network envelope remain open; 35-drone evidence cannot validate the late-game >10,000-enemy goal.

## Human comparison and decision

Use the same candidate/settings/60-HP fixture, seed, drone count and spawn rules for comparisons. Record exact release/source, participant, solo/pair, chosen card, level/selection/application times, HP/deaths, collected/uncollected XP, shots/hits/actual damage/kills by player, and any consistency/readability fault. A repeat seed controls initial conditions, not identical human movement or whole-game determinism.

1. Solo attempt: earn ten pickups normally, observe the level, select stronger hits, compare ordinary body/head hit requirements before/after and finish or die. Retry and choose faster fire. Reverse order on another participant/repeat if practical; do not pool learning/order effects into a balance conclusion.
2. Pair attempt: both earn the same opportunity from shared collection. Ask A to choose stronger hits and B faster fire as a comparison assignment, not a forced game rule. Observe A's choice leaving B's offer untouched and play complementary approaches to the same crowd. Retry with choices swapped; optionally observe both choosing the same card. Equal levels do not guarantee equal power or usefulness.
3. Ask each player: **Did you notice the change before checking the numbers? What became easier, and when was each option useful? Could you read threats and explain your teammate's contribution? Did selecting under pressure interrupt play or feel rewarding? Would you try the other choice?** Preserve their actual words and specific problems; automated outcomes do not answer enjoyment.
4. Separately exercise one death with a living collector/choice/return, an all-dead ending despite timers, solo death and shared retry reset. Use labelled diagnostic setup when necessary; do not present forced deaths/XP as ordinary play evidence.

Success means the action chain is complete and players can report a noticed change/usefulness while retaining useful threat/ally information. Failure to earn in time, distinguish effects, understand teammate damage or read cards is a useful revise result. Do not expand the pool/stack to hide it. The contract is ready for owner evaluation, **not owner accepted**: acceptance or one precise objection is the next required input. Save owner reasoning here and in GDJ-6 before treating the proposal as selected. Written acceptance still leaves human enjoyment/network feasibility untested.
