# 0009: Weapon control and accessible combat

- **Date:** 2026-10-03
- **Status:** accepted configurable auto-shoot, varied weapon targeting and through-wall enemy visibility direction; detailed activation/targeting/presentation rules remain open. Future positioning and high-priority readability research requested.

## Context

The owner supplied two Vampire Hunters settings screenshots while answering the weapon-control questions. They show separate primary/secondary/full-auto controls, constant-shoot and reload-related controls, global/category weapon-position settings, a merge option, enemy outlines with an X-Ray Blue value, detection, health bars and hit feedback. These are observations of the supplied interface, not verification of hidden behavior or selection of those exact settings for this game.

The owner wants an auto-shoot preference, weapon-specific crosshair/targeted/guided behavior, x-ray enemy visibility, future positioning experiments and highly valued deep research into readability/accessibility. This extends the readable, responsive combat pillar and preserves the [1-4 player bullet-heaven direction](0008-bullet-heaven-solo-play-and-core-experience.md).

## Accepted direction

- Offer a gameplay setting that enables automatic firing. Weapons fire automatically when that setting and their applicable firing rules permit it. Exact setting grouping, defaults, manual controls and cancellation are open. Auto-activation of passive effects and active secondary guns is a possible future extension, with its scope not selected yet.
- Weapons/items can have different aiming and targeting behaviors: follow the crosshair, choose targets, or guide an attack toward a particular target. Bosses, high-HP enemies, low-HP enemies and allies needing healing are owner-named targeting possibilities. These are supported design directions, not selected default priorities or an approved weapon roster.
- Enemies should be perceptible through walls using an x-ray/outline presentation. The owner proposes a human gift for detecting AI as its fiction. Default style, customization, range, filters and exceptions remain open. Visibility does not itself select penetration damage or target validity through obstacles.
- Experiment with weapon positioning later; no fixed mount/slot/merging layout is selected. Record this in [GDJ-11](https://linear.app/cnid/issue/GDJ-11/experiment-with-weapon-positioning-and-arsenal-visibility).
- Give screen readability and accessibility high design value and a deep future research task: [GDJ-12](https://linear.app/cnid/issue/GDJ-12/research-combat-readability-and-accessibility). Include visual and motor access needs, understandable upgrades, alternative sensory cues and performance costs. Accessibility is a core design consideration, not a result demonstrated by the current prototype.

## Fictional perception name remains open

The owner's suggested Alan Turing effect is a working fictional association, not a real vision phenomenon. Turing's [Computing Machinery and Intelligence](https://www.cse.msu.edu/~cse841/papers/Turing.html) describes the imitation game using written questions and answers to assess machine imitation; it does not describe x-ray perception. **Turing Sense** is a possible game name, unselected. Enemy outlines need no additional lore gate before they can be studied.

## Later owner clarification: first gun, growth and spectacle

Detailed first-gun design remains open. The owner selected a **steady rifle with predictable, frequent shots** as the archetype to explore first; this selects the design starting point, not the complete weapon specification. Define its baseline before choosing a concrete upgrade pool. The owner expects raw damage, area damage and other chosen effects to determine the usefulness and feel of growth. Changed bullets, trails and after-effects are possibilities rather than a selected visual treatment.

The screen should remain understandable as weapons accumulate. The owner also sees possible late-run chaos as part of the fun. Preserve useful threat/aim/ally information while investigating spectacle, comfort and performance; no readability or enjoyment result is established. GDJ-12 should include this tension in later research. The [encounter record](../design/engine-kingdoms-first-encounter.md#first-gun-and-readable-growth--discussion-checkpoint) contains the selected rifle archetype and proposed baseline/growth examples, and the [readability record](../design/weapon-control-and-combat-readability.md#owner-clarification-effect-dependent-growth-and-late-run-chaos) preserves these constraints.

## Consequences and later work

The [weapon-control and readability record](../design/weapon-control-and-combat-readability.md) separates firing activation, aiming/target choice, projectile guidance and visual arrangement. Automatic firing does not automatically choose upgrades or ready the player. The accepted thinking-space rule still prevents attacks while physical time is frozen.

Shared combat must agree on valid targets and effects while each player can choose presentation/input preferences. Investigate target filters, priority ties, HP interpretation, retargeting and healing applicability without choosing a networking package or a universal targeting engine here. Preserve dense-crowd performance and useful cues when visual effects are reduced.

GDJ-11 is future Backlog at Medium; GDJ-12 is future Backlog at High. Neither has a date or implementation commitment. Research recommendations such as outline thickness, redundant audio, remapping and narration remain proposals until scoped. Human observation with players who use access features is required; high FPS, rankings or screenshots cannot establish accessibility for everyone.

Update the foundation/current brief and existing Linear handoff, preserving the one-gun start, pleasant level-ups and performant core. Permanent matrix/power and optional cameras remain parked. No game implementation, Unity operations, benchmark or completed deep accessibility research occurs in this checkpoint.
